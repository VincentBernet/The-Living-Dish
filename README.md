# Blog Philosophy & Editorial Vision

This blog is about **understanding the world through food**.

Food is more than a collection of recipes. It is a reflection of a place, its history, its people, its resources, its traditions, and the way societies have evolved. A dish can tell us where people lived, what ingredients were available to them, what techniques they developed, who had access to certain foods, and how cultures influenced one another.

The blog explores cuisine as a form of **cultural storytelling**.

The author is a 27-year-old French food enthusiast living in Paris, who loves to travel and discover local cuisines. He is particularly interested in Japanese, Vietnamese, Thai and Asian cuisines in general, as well as Mediterranean cuisines such as Italian and Greek cuisine, and French cuisine.

The blog should reflect this personal perspective: **a curious traveler and cook trying to understand the food he encounters, rather than simply reproducing recipes.**

## Editorial Philosophy

Each article should aim to answer more than:

> "How do I cook this dish?"

It should also explore:

> "Where does this dish come from?"
> "Why was it created this way?"
> "Who traditionally ate it?"
> "What ingredients and techniques shaped it?"
> "What does it tell us about the culture that created it?"
> "How has it evolved over time?"
> "What makes this dish what it is?"

A recipe, when present, is therefore **the practical conclusion of a deeper exploration**, not necessarily the main purpose of the article.

The goal is for the reader to finish an article with three things:

1. **A better understanding of the dish or ingredient**
2. **A better understanding of the culture and history surrounding it**
3. **A new culinary technique, principle, or skill they can apply in their own kitchen**

## Content Beyond Recipes

Not every article needs to contain a recipe.

The blog can explore:

- The history and evolution of a dish
- Regional variations of the same food
- The origin and cultural importance of an ingredient
- Cooking techniques and why they work
- Fermentation, preservation, seasoning, and other culinary processes
- The influence of geography, climate, trade, religion, social class, or migration on cuisine
- Comparisons between culinary traditions
- The evolution of traditional dishes into their modern forms

For example, an article could explore why dashi became fundamental to Japanese cuisine, how pho developed in Vietnam, why acidity plays such an important role in Thai cuisine, or how French culinary techniques influenced other food cultures.

## The Role of Travel

Travel is an important part of the blog's perspective.

Discovering a cuisine should be treated as a way of discovering a place. Local dishes, ingredients, markets, restaurants, traditions, and cooking techniques can reveal aspects of a culture that are difficult to understand through history or geography alone.

The blog should therefore encourage **culinary exploration**: traveling through recipes, ingredients, techniques, and stories.

However, the goal is not to present an exoticized or romanticized version of foreign cuisines. Cultural context should be treated with curiosity, respect, and nuance.

## The Culinary Approach

The blog should combine:

**Culture** — understanding where food comes from.

**History** — understanding how it evolved.

**Technique** — understanding how and why it is cooked.

**Ingredients** — understanding their role, origin, and properties.

**Practice** — giving the reader the knowledge needed to reproduce and adapt what they have learned.

The writing should favor **depth over quantity**. A recipe should not exist simply to fill a recipe page. Every recipe, ingredient, or technique should have something worth understanding behind it.

## Core Principle

The underlying philosophy of the blog can be summarized as:

> **Understand what you eat, so you can better understand how to cook it.**

Or, more broadly:

> **Travel through food. Understand through taste.**

The blog should ultimately make readers more curious about what they eat — and give them the knowledge and techniques to become better cooks themselves.
