steps:
  - Peel your kilo of cooked shrimp<a id="footnote-ref-steps" href="#recipe-steps-note" class="recipe-footnote-reference" aria-label="See the peeling note">*</a>, set the shrimp aside, keeping the heads & shells separate.
  - Take your pot (saucepan / sauté pan, as long as it has plenty of room), heat it over high heat, once it is hot, add your olive oil then sear your shells, stirring regularly.
  - A few min later, deglaze with your glass of dry white wine then make a little room in your pot, add a little olive oil again, and sweat your shallots and onions for 5 min, add the Espelette pepper.
  - Bonus => add a splash of cognac and flambé it.
  - Return your shells, add water to cover, add your bay leaf & a little tomato paste.
  - Reduce your stock by half over medium heat, for about 30 min.
  - Now pass it through a chinois, gently pressing the shells to extract as much stock as possible.
  - Finally you can pour this precious liquid into an ice cube tray to use later, take care not to fill the compartments to the brim as the liquid takes up more space when frozen!

## A stock that's very simple to make and helps reduce food waste

When you buy cooked shrimp<a id="footnote-ref-crustaces-prose" href="#footnote-crustaces" class="recipe-footnote-reference" aria-label="Shrimp, see note">\*</a> (or raw ones), don't throw away the heads and shells, they're a flavour bomb for your future dishes.
Here we'll see how to make shrimp stock, a reduced & flavourful broth all right, but how do you use it?

Shrimp stock (like shellfish or fish stock), is one of the basic kitchen stocks, preparations that are never an end in themselves, but are used to season sauces, condiments, main courses and second courses.
In classic French cooking stocks play a fundamental role, it's no coincidence that they're called fonds. They are the foundations, so if those are good, your dishes will also stand a good chance of being good.
For shellfish stock for example one or two ladles and your sauce will take on a whole new dimension, your risotto will soak up umami or your paella will have a flavour that keeps you coming back for more.

Here unlike a meat stock (chicken stock, beef stock etc) it's a really simple recipe, much quicker (from three to ten hours for beef stock to half an hour for shellfish stock). You don't need all the ingredients,
just shrimp shells and a little good olive oil will do. Aromatics such as shallots, garlic or bay leaves are a bonus,
absolutely not essential, though welcome. For an extra treat you can also add a splash of cognac and flambé it.

Once finished you can simply keep this broth as ice cubes in your freezer for 3 to 4 weeks. In the refrigerator 2 or 3 days but not much longer.
Here are a few recipes to use this precious stock:

- A shrimp risotto
- Natin / Nataing (a Cambodian dish, a mixture of pork, shrimp, and coconut cream + peanuts, absolutely delicious, a recipe is coming soon)
- A paella
- Or in a multitude of sauces to accompany your shellfish or fish
