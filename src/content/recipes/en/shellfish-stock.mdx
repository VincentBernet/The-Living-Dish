steps:
  - Peel your kilo of cooked shrimp*, set the shrimp aside, keeping the heads & shells separate.
  - Take your pot (saucepan / sauté pan, as long as it has plenty of room), heat it over high heat, once it is hot, add your olive oil then sear your shells, stirring regularly.
  - Deglaze with your glass of dry white wine, let it evaporate for about 15 s, then set this liquid and the shells aside.
  - In the same pot add a little olive oil again and sweat your shallots and onions for 5 min, add the Espelette pepper.
  - Bonus => add a splash of cognac and flambé it.
  - Return your shells, add water to cover, add your bay leaf & a little tomato paste.
  - Reduce your stock by half over medium heat, for about 30 min.
  - Now pass it through a chinois, gently pressing the shells to extract as much stock as possible.
  - Finally you can pour this precious liquid into an ice cube tray, take care not to fill the compartments to the brim (as the liquid takes up more space when frozen), so you can use it whenever you like!

stepsNote:

      * As for the peeling technique, pinch at the base of the head to detach it from the body, then remove the two upper shell segments and the legs in one motion, then the two lower segments in another, then finally pinch the tail while holding the body to detach the last two segments. After a kilo you should start getting the hang of it.

## A stock that's very simple to make and helps reduce food waste

When you buy cooked shrimp (or raw ones), don't throw away the heads and shells, they're a flavour bomb for your future dishes.
Here we'll see how to make shrimp stock, a reduced broth packed with flavour, all right but how do you use it?

Shrimp stock (like shellfish or fish stock), is one of the basic kitchen stocks, preparations that are never an end in themselves, but are used to season sauces, condiments, main courses and second courses.
One or two ladles and your sauce will take on a whole new dimension, your risotto will soak up umami or your paella will have a flavour that keeps you coming back for more.

It's a really simple recipe, you don't need all the ingredients,
just shrimp shells and a little good olive oil will do. Aromatics such as shallots, garlic or bay leaves are a bonus,
absolutely not essential. For an extra treat you can also add a splash of cognac and flambé it.

Once finished you can simply keep this broth as ice cubes in your freezer for 3 to 4 weeks. In the refrigerator 2 or 3 days but not much longer.
