steps:
  - Finely chop the onion, carrot, and celery to make the soffritto.
  - Sauté the soffritto in olive oil over low heat for 10 minutes, until soft and translucent.
  - Add minced garlic, then the ground meat, and cook while breaking it up until well browned.
  - Pour in the red wine, let it evaporate, then stir in the tomato paste and crushed tomatoes.
  - Season with salt and pepper, cover, and simmer over very low heat for at least 2 hours, stirring regularly.
  - Add the milk halfway through cooking, stir, and continue until the sauce is thick and homogeneous.

## Bologna's ragù

Bolognese sauce — or ragù alla bolognese — comes from Bologna, the capital of Emilia-Romagna, a region renowned for its generous cuisine and fresh pasta. Unlike the popular image of a bright red, tomato-heavy sauce, the authentic ragù is a long-simmered meat preparation where tomato plays a supporting role.

Its secret lies in the soffritto, the trio of finely chopped onion, carrot, and celery cooked gently until meltingly soft — the aromatic foundation of much Italian cooking. In 1982, the Accademia Italiana della Cucina even registered an official recipe with the Bologna Chamber of Commerce, proof of how deeply Bolognese cherish their tradition.

In Italy, it is traditionally served with fresh tagliatelle, never with spaghetti. It is the slow cooking — two hours or more — that breaks down the meat, rounds the flavors, and lets the milk soften the tomato's acidity. A dish that looks simple, but demands patience.
