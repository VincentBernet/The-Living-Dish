steps:
  - "*Mise en place*: Finely mince the shallots and cut the butter into small cubes, then set it aside in the refrigerator."
  - "Add the shallots, white wine, and vinegar to a saucepan, then lightly season with salt. Bring to a boil over high heat, then reduce the heat to low or medium-low to maintain a gentle simmer."
  - "Reduce until only a quarter of the original volume remains. You can either leave the shallots in or strain the reduction through a *chinois* (fine-mesh sieve) for a perfectly smooth sauce. The choice is yours; if you leave the shallots in, just make sure they are cooked through."
  - "Return the reduction to very low heat and add the white pepper. Gradually whisk in the butter cubes, a small amount at a time. Let each cube almost completely melt and emulsify before adding the next batch. If you keep the saucepan over very low heat, the butter will not split* and the emulsion will form. Season with additional salt and pepper to taste. To get it just right, remember one thing: taste, taste, taste."

stepsNote:
  - text: >-
      * Keep the temperature low throughout, as the sauce must not boil or the emulsion may split. Fat and water do not naturally mix. The whisk's mechanical action incorporates air, and together with the temperature difference between the butter and the reduction, this creates an emulsion: the fat disperses into tiny droplets throughout the water-based liquid.
